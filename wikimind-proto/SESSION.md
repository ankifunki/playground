## Part 1: Agentic sysadmin product ("Warden")

1. **What sysadmins do and their pain points.** Alert fatigue, interrupt-driven work, patch fear, tool sprawl, access sprawl, documentation rot. The leverage is in repetitive, manual work.
2. **"Build an agent, a digital human, to address this."** Pushback: sysadmins buy a scoped, auditable agent with a verifiable blast radius, not a persona. Proposed wedge: read-only alert triage for MSPs, then access lifecycle.
3. **"What if the agent *is* the sysadmin?"** Reframed as an AI-native MSP selling outcomes to 10–200 person companies with no IT staff. Introduced the autonomy ladder L0 observe → L1 propose → L2 auto on an allowlist → L3 auto in a window → L4. Risks: prompt injection through tickets, compounding errors, liability.
4. **UX for configuring behavior.** A policy and trust console: capability matrix (system × action × level), plain-English policy compiled to deterministic rules, shadow-mode replay, approval inbox, audit timeline, boundaries, an always-visible kill switch. Promotion is evidence-gated; demotion is automatic.
5. Inline wireframes of the capability matrix with a promote-to-L2 flow, then the demotion and anomaly flow, plus a global circuit breaker.
6. **Full console artifact** → `archive/01-warden-console.html`.
7. **"Ruthless simplicity, NL and voice first, expose only what's needed."** Flipped from cockpit to exception handler → `archive/02-warden-chat.html`. One ask at a time, agent-initiated promotions, stop always one tap.
8. **"Expose the agent's brain so a human can mutate behavior."** Brain as external, legible layers (hard bounds, rules, lessons, environment facts, taste) with different mutation rights. Learning may change *how*, never *what*. Added a "what have you learned?" flow with retire, fork, and rollback.
9. **Multiple users, few governors.** Roles: requester, domain approver, governor, auditor. Requesters are an evidence stream, not authority. Confused-deputy risk, approval laundering, governor attention budget.
10. **Fleet management** with systemic updates such as onboarding a vendor → `archive/03-warden-fleet.html` (agent strip, canary rollout, revoke everywhere, fleet rules).
11. **Unique visual identity per agent plus proactivity** → Iris (IT), Sol (Security), Fen (Finance), Pia (People). Proactive notes in three kinds (needs you, will do unless you say no, for your info), each citing what it learned from, under an attention budget.
12. **"How Iris works" brain page**: job, playbooks, access, hard rules, learned habits, knowledge, changes. Edit by tapping a line or by sentence. Widening goes to a second approver; narrowing applies instantly.
13. **Audit trace per unit of work**: work log plus trace view (trigger, read, checked, did, result), cross-agent links, undo.
14. **More human visual style**: Figtree, fog palette, soft cards, a greeting line.
15. **Push the brain visualization**: radial brain map with a "watch it handle a task" walkthrough (sense, understand, check, act, ask, learn), plus five operating-profile dials (autonomy, precision, reach, proactivity, learning) as a radar with last-week impact previews.
16. **"Is this the right question?"** Honest critique: feature accretion, designing in a vacuum. Reframe: the governor's job is to delegate safely, catch mistakes cheaply, and stay accountable. Core object becomes the **decision class**, and people correct on examples rather than editing rules.
17. **Warden v2** → `variants/warden-v2.html`: decision classes with agreement dots, spot checks, correct-on-example that generalizes and shows impact.
18. Built the variant switcher. Removed the conversation variant at Ankit's request.

## Part 2: Wikimind (Asoka design on-site brief)

19. **Brief** (see CLAUDE.md for the link): read-only living knowledge base, the proof layer for buyers. Goals: trust, alive, AI-native, beautiful. Specs: Page and Home.
20. **Wikimind v1** → `variants/wikimind.html`: home constellation and live ticker, page with per-block trust tiers, git-blame rail, a live update when a question gets answered, citation landing.
21. **"Verification isn't necessary up front, just easy to reach."** → trust by exception: only uncertain, changed, unofficial, or stale content is marked. A one-tap "why" with the full trail one step further, and a "How Asoka knows this" lens for demos.
22. **"Actionable focus, why am I here and what can I do."** → arrival modes (Slack citation, home story, browsing, sales demo), each with a context card and actions. Added the relationship spider graph and accordion sections.
23. **Polish pass** using Emil Kowalski's design-engineering skills (found via X). Fixed real bugs: the Android tap highlight, clipped rows, and **CSS class collisions** (`.x`, `.ck`, `.cite`). Rebuilt the stats as a grid.
24. **Product critique, argued as a builder.** It was a wiki with trust tags. Novel directions: claims not pages, why-it's-like-this, policy vs. practice, time travel, "what breaks," showing the unknown. Orientation: two modes (Ask and Map), semantic zoom, two trust states.
25. **Wikimind 2** → `variants/wikimind2.html`: Ask-first home, semantic-zoom map (company → area → process → claim), claims with causes, time-travel slider, policy vs. practice, blast radius. Then made end-to-end: 17 processes with data, live Claude answers grounded in claims with a local fallback, pinch/pan/wheel zoom with snap, cross-area dependency links and fly-to.
26. **Neumorphism experiment: rejected.** Ankit's rule: never use that aesthetic for anything.
27. **"Familiar to wikis for the buyer"** → Wikimind 3 with a Wikipedia look (`archive/04-wikimind3-wiki-look-rejected.html`). **Rejected:** "a step backward, nowhere near wiki look and feel." Restyled into the house language, wiki vocabulary renamed (Overview, Questions, Changes, Sources, In focus, What changed, Unwritten rules Asoka found).
28. **Persistent bottom composer** with attachments, voice (browser speech recognition), a scope toggle (this page or all of Northwind), and a chat panel → current `variants/wikimind3.html`. Pre-composer version: `archive/05-wikimind3-before-composer.html`.
29. **`stageshow` skill** (`npx skills add benjitaylor/skills --skill stageshow`): couldn't install from claude.ai (no network) and not found in public indexes. Try it here.
30. Moved to Claude Code.

## Part 3: Claude Code

31. **Hardening Wikimind 3, then React.** Answers lead with the single answer (the 50% renewal case was wrong). "I don't know" offers "Ask <name>" or "Not now", with Undo, and the person answers back (presenter keys `L` and `A`). The learning moment became visible: chip, toast, and a struck-then-rewritten renewal card. Show-on-page from answers, scope toggle removed, keyboard and focus fixes, icon buttons for secondary actions. Then the whole prototype moved to Vite + React + TypeScript, every variant ported with browser checks. Geist for body, Geist Mono for tags, warm stone base color, + and − map zoom in Wikimind 2. Hosted for preview on Vercel behind a password page.
32. **Home and pages anchor on trust vectors.** The Home stats (pages, observations, % verified) were upfront but led nowhere. Home now orients toward building trust along three vectors: reliability, automation (progress toward full automation), and gaps and access (what blocks it). New hero: "What you can trust Asoka with today, and what it needs to do more." A path-to-full-automation bar (runs on its own / asks a person first / blocked by gaps or access) sits above three cards. Reliability: 98.6% matched, 1,912 decisions in 30 days, 3 reversals you can open. Automation: 64% (up from 51% in September), with "Hand it over" for license seats, which goes to Dev for a second OK and moves the bar to 66%. Gaps and access: 7% blocked, with "Grant" for Okta sign-in logs (4%) and NetSuite write (2%), and "Follow" for the flat-renewals question. The split is derived, never stored. Scale numbers moved into the "How Asoka keeps this current" card. On the page, "At a glance" leads with three trust rows (reliability, automation, gaps) instead of the statements-verified bar. In React: `Trust.tsx`, state `tv` in the store, Grant also goes through Dev like any widening.
33. **Visual polish and one cut.** The path-to-automation bar became evenly spaced 2px vertical bars that rise in on load and recolour in a wave. Every Wikimind 3 visual now shares one fine line language: 1px hairlines for structure, 2px square bars for data, 1.5px for trends. "How Asoka keeps this current" was removed from Home: each loop step (observe, update, verify, ask) is already shown live by the feed, What changed, the trust cards, and Asoka is asking, so the diagram only explained what the page already demonstrates. The scale line moved under "Learning right now"; the open-questions count joined the "Asoka is asking" heading.

34. **Wikimind 4: provenance in the margin, answers in place.** A review of Wikimind 3 against the goals found trust strong but AI-native weak: the page was still built like a wiki (contents rail, facts box, numbered citations, History, Related and Sources sections) and answers lived in a separate chat. Wikimind 4 (`?v=g`, now the default) keeps Wikimind 3 and changes five things. (1) Every sentence carries its own provenance: hover or focus it and the margin shows each source, whether a person checked it, and what it replaced; a click pins it; on phones a tap opens the same card in the sheet. Only changed or uncertain sentences carry a margin mark ("Changed Oct 28", "Seen once"); on narrow screens the mark sits inline. The numbered citations, Sources, History, Related, contents rail, facts box and Questions/Changes tabs are gone: related pages are links inside sentences, changes live on the sentence, the open question lives in its card in In practice, and three trust rows sit under the title. (2) Arriving from Slack (`#approvals-slack`, key `S`, or the Demo link on Home) leads with the question and answer and marks the two sentences behind it. (3) No chat: an answer is written into the page beside the sentences it rests on, unknowns land in In practice with Ask/Not now/Undo, and the person's reply arrives in the same block; on Home answers sit under the hero. (4) A live "Right now" line under the hero, and Maria answers by herself about 20 seconds after the page opens. (5) Home has one "What's moving" feed (changes, reading, asking) beside a text-only In focus; unwritten rules and Areas sit below.

35. **Wikimind 4: icons, room, less copy.** Sources show as an overlapping pile of source-kind icons (Slack, Salesforce, Jira, policy doc, meeting, watched, person), with a green dot when a person verified them, in answers, the arrival card, the margin note, the sheet and the page footer. Feed rows on Home lead with icons too (changed, asking, answered, source kind). Section nav came back as icons with labels (Summary, How it works, Rules, In practice, Systems): a sticky rail at 1240px and wider, sticky chips below; each icon moves like its idea on hover (lines write, nodes connect, the check draws, the eye blinks, the plug seats), hover-only and off under reduced motion. Cards got 24px padding, Home sections 48px apart, page headings 64px. Explanatory copy was cut: the Home subline, the page byline, figure and replay captions, the In practice intro, the margin hint, and wordy trust-card and feed lines.

36. **The same pass on Wikimind 3.** The numbered citations became small icon piles after each sentence (tap opens every source behind it in the sheet, with its icon, quote and verified/unchecked state). The Sources list leads each entry with its icon, the Home feed with icons. The contents rail and the Overview/Questions/Changes tabs got icons that animate on hover (History's clock hand turns, Related's link joins, Sources' layers lift); on phones the "On this page" dropdown became sticky icon chips. Same airy spacing and the same copy cuts as Wikimind 4.

37. **Asoka is a team, not one agent.** Correction from the brief's "digital human": Asoka is a set of specialist agents, each with one job. Wikimind 3 and 4 now credit every fact, change, question and answer to its agent: Approvals, Access, Security, Finance ops, Integrations, Support (square indigo tiles; sources stay round and neutral). The header shows the team ("6 agents working"); Home's hero reads "What you can trust the agents with today, and what they need to do more"; a new "The agents" card lists each agent's job, reliability, share on its own and what holds it back (asking someone, missing access), and Hand it over / Grant update the right agent. Feed rows, misses, unwritten rules, the page byline ("Kept by Approvals agent"), the margin note, the source sheet, the systems table ("Watched by"), Questions threads and answers all name their agent. Unknown questions: "No agent knows that yet, and none will guess", and the agent for that team does the asking. Also fixed: "Show on page" highlights could be wiped by a hover re-render in Wikimind 4; the highlight is now React state.

38. **Type restraint.** Tags read too big and bold. Wikimind 3 and 4 now use two weights only: 400 for reading and 500 for emphasis and titles (no 600/700 anywhere; `<b>` is 500). Geist Serif stays at 400 so the browser never fakes a bold. Every tag, pill, label and kicker is Geist Mono 11px at 450 with a little tracking. Considered and declined for now: `@polishd/next` (Next.js-only analytics; this app is Vite).

39. **Wikimind 5, from the brief itself.** Ankit shared the full brief as screenshots (the Google Doc isn't readable from Claude Code); its essentials are now in CLAUDE.md. Built a new variant (`?v=h`, default) around it. **Home** is a front page between Wikipedia and the New York Times: a dated edition ("Edition updated 4s ago"), a lead story on the biggest Salesforce change with a threshold diagram, a pausable live wire of what each agent is doing, the whole corpus (1,284 pages) as a grid coloured by trust tier where squares light up as agents update them, and Changed / Learned / Asking columns. **The job page** is Discount approvals, chosen because the brief's business-user example is exactly an agent pushing back in Slack and citing a policy page: an evidence lens (every sentence underlined by tier with its sources inline), a trust line (checked by a person, last observed, cited by agents), a "Try a deal" simulator generated from the page's rules and the live config (deal size, discount, deal type, EMEA, quarter end → who approves and how long, then a route diagram, a mock Salesforce record and captions that play step by step with restart, back, play/pause, forward, scrubber, speed and arrow keys, each step citing its rule), "How it's configured" for architects (the Opportunity object and the five automations that touch it, why each exists, who watches it), and receipts (agent actions that cited the page, with "Because" quotes that light up the rule). **Alive:** a receipt arrives, then Priya changes Finance review in Salesforce: the agent rewrites the sentence (20% struck, 25% written in), withdraws Dana's verification, the simulator and answers follow the new threshold, Home's lead story is replaced, and Dana re-verifies seconds later. **Arrival from Slack** shows Jamie's request and the agent's pushback, with "Try your deal" loading their 40% deal into the simulator. **A 7-step tour** for the CIO maps to the brief: assembled automatically, at a glance, proof not claims, anyone can see how it works, keeps itself current, closes its own gaps, what agents cite. Caught one more class collision (`.obs` tier vs the `.obs` card); tier classes are now prefixed.

## Open threads worth picking up

- "Hand it over" and "Grant" should only render for governors. Other users see the trust vectors without actions.
- Trust-vector numbers are hard-coded demo data.
- Apply the stageshow skill to Wikimind 3.
- Fold Questions and Changes into the page itself (inline threads, time slider) if separate views still feel wiki-adjacent.
- Bring Wikimind 2's semantic-zoom map into Wikimind 3's home as the way to browse areas.
- Real attachment reading and a real retrieval model instead of keyword matching.
- Test with real users: "Would you trust this page?" and time-to-answer for architects.
