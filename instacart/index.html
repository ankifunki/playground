A problem upstream

Customer reporting flow

The customer flow produced incomplete data, and didn’t give people the controls to report accurately.

Key challenges

Connecting the dots. Zooming out on each challenge showed one systemic issue for appeasement.

Internal problem

Lack of structured, specific issue data

The ML team wasn’t getting clean, reliable data to train its models.

Business problem

An inefficient, expensive process

Classification was prone to human error and expensive in time.

User problem

Limited taxonomy and reporting

People lacked the controls to report the specifics of an issue.

Customer mental model

Intuitive reporting. We validated with users how they think about reporting, to set the hierarchy and flow.

  1. What went wrong with the order?General issue
  2. Which items had an issue?Items with issues
  3. Specifically, what is the issue?Sub-issue
  4. How severe was it?Quantity affected
  5. Based on what you reportedResolution

Initial UX direction

Prototypes
  • Report multiple issues at once
  • An aggregated resolution amount is better perceived
  • Still prone to gaming the system
  • Added engineering complexity
  • A focused flow for one issue
  • The resolution amount is relatively small
  • A single-item flow makes it abuse-proof
  • Straightforward to build

Divergent directions: two prototypes with debatable trade-offs, to bring foundational alignment.

Weights & measures

Mild to wild
Tell us whether you received more or less.
Step the weight you received, in 0.25 lb increments.
Scan the item’s barcode to detect what arrived.

Capturing severity for weight-based items. Some produce and meat is ordered by count but sold by weight, so people need dynamic controls to report it.

Final validation

14 concept tests · survey n=1,815

With UXR, we put the final prototype in front of real Instacart customers. They already find reporting easy, so they’re excited for the improvements.

  1. Most prefer self-serveThe majority of customers would rather report an issue themselves.
  2. Some issues lean to CareMissing items, incorrect items and poor replacements were more likely to prefer Care.
  3. Direct and simpleCustomers are looking for directness and simplicity.
  4. Easy and quickThe issue-specific flow is very easy to navigate, with a few minor usability tweaks to fix.
