How they connect

A business offers services.

Each is provided by staff who can do it.

Only on the days they’re scheduled.

Service, staff and an open day meet in one slot: a booking.

With two locations every layer splits, except staff. Selina spans both, and each location gets its own days and calendar.

What made it hard

  1. Platform shiftCut across every surface. Most workflows had to be rethought.
  2. Every teamEvery domain had a stake, and its own edge cases to learn.
  3. Moving targetA rebrand ran in parallel, so the design system shifted underfoot.
  4. Two platformsDesktop and mobile parity, native to each. Mobile was legacy code on an old design system.
  5. No clear shapeVague, broad and complex. Everyone had an idea; nothing was tangible.

Making it tangible

Everyone pictured something different. Prototypes built with AI from first-principles needs gave the team one thing to react to, and put it in front of users early.

Manage globally. Operate locally.

Information architecture

Current

Business, then workflows

New

Business, locations, then workflows

Switching locations

One switcher for the whole app. Every view is locked to one location, and owners lose the overview.

A global switcher plus one per workflow. Two switchers with conflicting logic.

Each workflow carries its own switcher, e.g. Staff at East Village. Works for owners and operators at any scale.
