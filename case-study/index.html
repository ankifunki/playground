How they connect

Services sit in a tray, each block as tall as its price.

A stylist’s schedule slides onto the calendar board.

Shutters open on the days they work. Only those slots can be booked.

A service drops through an open day into a slot: a booking. One business, one board.

Add a location and a second board drops in. Selina’s week splits between them, and the same haircut costs more in Soho.

What made it hard

  1. Platform shiftCut across every surface. Most workflows had to be rethought.
  2. Every teamTrue cross-functional work. I learned each domain to solve it.
  3. Moving targetA rebrand ran in parallel, so the design system shifted underfoot.
  4. Two platformsDesktop and mobile parity, native to each. Mobile was legacy code on an old design system.
  5. No clear shapeVague, broad and complex. Everyone had an idea; nothing was tangible.

Making it tangible

I prototyped with AI from first-principles user needs, to align the team and put something real in front of users early.

Core needs

Owner

Manage the whole business

  • Clients across locations
  • Sales across locations
  • Business-wide analytics
Operator

Run each location

  • Book at a location
  • Schedule per location
  • Inventory per location

Principle

Manage globally. Operate locally.

Information architecture

Current

Business, then workflows

New

Business, locations, then workflows

Switching locations

One switcher for the whole app. Every view is locked to one location, and owners lose the overview.

A global switcher plus one per workflow. Two switchers with conflicting logic.

Each workflow carries its own switcher, e.g. Team at Soho. Works for owners and operators at any scale.

Shared patterns

  1. Default, then override per location
  2. Assign people and services to locations
  3. [Surface] at [Location]
  1. Bulk by default
  2. Scales to 20+ locations
