Case study · GlossGenius

Multi Location

GlossGenius was built for a salon or spa with one front door. Supporting businesses with many locations meant redesigning every primary workflow in the product, because each one quietly assumed there was only one.

RoleLead product designer
TimelineApr – Oct 2025
ScopeLocations, staff, services, settings, calendar, clients

01 · The challenge

One assumption, built into everything

Booking an appointment, inviting a team member, pricing a service, setting a cancellation policy: every workflow was designed for a single location. Multi Location was the path upmarket, but it couldn't ship as one feature. Location had to become a new dimension that ran across the whole product.

02 · The approach

Set the rules before drawing the screens

Owners and operators pull in different directions once a business has more than one location. Owners ask how to manage the business as a whole. Operators ask how to run the location they're standing in. Every surface had to answer both, so I started with a principle, a new information architecture and a small set of patterns, and applied them everywhere.

Manage globally, with flexibility to operate locally.

Where the location switcher lives

This was the first big decision, since every later screen depended on it. I explored three models and reviewed them against four jobs: always knowing which location you're in, moving a workflow between locations, seeing one location's content, and giving owners a central view.

Global

Pick a location once; everything shows that location.

  • Familiar pattern
  • Restricts every view to one location
  • No central view for owners
  • Doesn't scale past a few locations
Chosen Workflow level

Each workflow has its own location switcher, e.g. "Team at Soho".

  • Business and location switchers stay distinct
  • Works for owners and operators
  • UI scales regardless of location count
Hybrid

A global switcher plus a switcher per workflow.

  • Purpose of the global switcher is unclear
  • Its logic conflicts with switching inside a workflow
  • An extra step with short-lived value

03 · Surface by surface

Before and after

Each surface below uses the same principle and patterns. Green marks steps that are new for multi-location.

M1 · Location management

Add and set up a location

Add a locationSet it up from an existing locationBring it online fully set up
Basics→Hours, prefilled→Specialty, prefilled→Services, in bulk→Staff, in bulk→Copy settings→Confirm plan→Location created
  • Opening a second location starts from the first. Hours and specialty come prefilled, and services and staff carry over in bulk.
  • Settings and preferences can be copied from another location instead of being set up from scratch.
  • The subscription change is shown before confirming (for example, $48 to $96 a month), so the cost is never a surprise.

M2 · Service management

Creating and pricing services

Offer a service at some locationsPrice it per locationCustomize price and duration per member
Default price & duration→Choose locations→Choose staff at those locations→Price per member
  • A service is created once for the business, then switched on per location. No duplicate menus.
  • Only members assigned to a location appear when choosing staff, which keeps long lists manageable.
  • Per-member pricing shows the final price clients will see on the booking site.
  • The services list shows "Offered at 1+ locations", and the location picker searches and truncates for businesses with 20+ locations.

M3 · Staff management

Inviting and managing the team

Assign a member to locationsSet their schedule per locationMake them bookable online

Before: owners couldn't assign a location when inviting a member, schedules couldn't vary by location, and members couldn't set their own availability per location.

Add members→Bulk invite→Roles & permissions→Assign locations→Schedule per location→Assign services
  • Locations and per-location schedules are part of the invite flow, so new members are bookable from day one.
  • "Staff at [Location]" filters the team, and each member shows where and when they work.
  • Permissions gained location settings, and roles can be customized for one member or many at once.

M4 · Settings management

Standardizing settings across locations

Standardize settings across locationsOverride a setting for one locationControl who can change what
  • Each setting shows whether all locations match or some differ, and owners can update across locations in one step.
  • Members with access to a location can adjust its settings. A gentle, non-blocking warning appears when they move away from the business standard.
  • Access follows the role: owners manage global settings, and staff see only their assigned locations.

Informed by a competitive review of Boulevard, Fresha and other salon platforms, which split business-level and location-level settings in different ways.

M6 · Booking & client management

Calendar, booking and clients

View my schedule at another locationBook at a different locationOne client list across locations
  • The calendar gets its own location switcher, with a live preview of another location's calendar while booking.
  • Appointments carry their location through to the client's confirmation email.
  • Clients stay in one shared list, filterable by location, with appointment history tagged by location.

04 · Outcome

Opening the door to bigger businesses

The business goal was to enable upmarket, multi-location businesses on GlossGenius and grow the share of customers on the higher plans.

To add

What shipped, adoption or plan-upgrade numbers, and customer feedback (for example, the member list update on 09/05 that came from user feedback).

What I learned

When a change runs horizontally across a product, the shared decisions matter more than any single screen. Settling the principle, the information architecture and the switcher model first let each later milestone move quickly and stay consistent with the rest.
